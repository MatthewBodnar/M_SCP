# Introduction to marketing and industrial marketing

Campus Tees prints shirts for student clubs. Use this business in each example.

## Marketing, needs, wants and demands

**Lecture slides 2–5. Exact marketing definition:**

> A social and managerial process by which individuals and groups obtain what they need and want through creating and exchanging products and value with others.

**Plain English:** find out what people need. Offer something useful in exchange for money or other value.

| Term | Exact lecture wording | Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Need | A state of felt deprivation. | A club needs its team to feel united. |
| Want | The form that a human need takes as shaped by culture and individual personality. | The club wants orange shirts with its logo. |
| Demand | Human wants that are backed by purchasing power. | The club wants 100 shirts and can pay for them. |

**Plain English:** a need is something missing. A want is the chosen solution. Demand means the buyer can pay.

## Industrial marketing and derived demand

**Slide 6. Exact definition:**

> Marketing of goods and services to individuals and organizations for purposes other than personal consumption

**Plain English:** sell to an organisation for its work, rather than for someone's private use.

**Campus Tees:** selling volunteer shirts to a club is a business purchase. Selling one shirt for personal use is a consumer purchase.

**Derived demand:** a business buys inputs because its customers need the final product. More club orders lead Campus Tees to buy more blank shirts.

**Slide 7:** business markets often have fewer, larger buyers. Buyers may check technical details, involve several people and keep long supplier relationships.

## Segmentation, targeting and positioning

**Slide 8. Exact definition of segmentation:**

> To divide a market into distinct groups of buyers with different needs, characteristics or behaviors, who might require separate products or marketing mixes.

**Plain English:** put buyers with similar needs into useful groups.

**Slide 9: six steps.**

| Step | What Campus Tees does |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Identify segmentation bases. | Group buyers by order size, use or delivery needs. |
| 2. Develop segment profiles. | Describe clubs, sports teams and local firms. |
| 3. Measure segment attractiveness. | Compare demand, likely profit and the cost of serving each group. |
| 4. Select target segments. | Choose clubs ordering 100–500 shirts. |
| 5. Develop positioning. | Be known for reliable event shirts and simple ordering. |
| 6. Develop the marketing mix. | Offer suitable shirts, prices, ordering channels and promotion. |

[diagram:stp]

**Keep the difference clear:** segmentation finds the groups. Targeting chooses which groups to serve. Positioning explains why those buyers should choose the firm.

The marketing mix covers **product, price, place and promotion**. Campus Tees must have enough shirts and printing time to keep its delivery promise.
