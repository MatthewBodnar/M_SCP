# Negotiations

## Two types of negotiation

**Slides 3–6 and Larsson (2020).** Negotiation helps people reach a shared decision when their needs differ. A good negotiator finds the other side's needs and explains their own.

| Type | Lecture idea | Simple Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distributive | A fixed pie to divide: win–lose. | With all other terms fixed, a lower price gives the supplier less money. |
| Integrative | Expand the pie: win–win. | A steady order plan helps the supplier cut costs and offer better delivery. |

[diagram:negotiationpie]

A deal can include both types. First find a better way to work; then agree how to share the benefit.

## Prepare: objectives and BATNA

**Slides 8–9.** Gather facts, rank needs, set goals and understand the other side.

**Exact BATNA expansion:**

> best alternative to a negotiated agreement

**Plain English:** your best real option if you do not reach a deal.

**Campus Tees:** Supplier B can provide suitable blanks on time. Using B is the BATNA. A maximum acceptable price is a limit, not the alternative itself.

Set three outcomes: **worst acceptable, reasonable/fair, and best possible**. Compare total price, quality and delivery. Do not claim a backup exists if it does not.

## Seven negotiation phases

**Slide 7 and slides 10–15.**

| Phase | Main task | Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparations | Gather facts and set goals. | Check price, stock and backup supply. |
| Greeting | Build a useful working relationship. | Show interest and listen. |
| Introduction | Agree on the purpose and agenda. | List price, quantity and delivery issues. |
| Argumentation | Give reasons and understand needs. | Explain why Friday delivery matters. |
| Compromise | Trade issues in a complete package. | Offer steady volume for reliable weekly deliveries. |
| Closure | Write down the full agreement. | Confirm sizes, dates, price and responsibilities. |
| Realisation | Carry out and check the agreement. | Send the promised order plan and check deliveries. |

[diagram:phases]

**From Larsson's paper:** a signed deal is not the end. Realisation matters because the agreed work must happen. It also affects the next negotiation.

## Trade issues together

Campus Tees wants a good price and reliable supply. The supplier wants steady volume and fewer urgent changes. Agree on a package that links these needs.

Do not accept each demand separately. Check the whole offer. Stay calm, avoid ultimatums and never lie.

## Culture: ask rather than assume

**Slides 17–22:** culture can affect time, trust and how people reach an agreement. The slides contrast a Swedish focus on plans and contracts with a Chinese focus on relationships, face and reciprocity.

These are broad teaching patterns, not rules for every person. Ask what matters to this supplier. Listen, check that both sides understand, and show respect.
