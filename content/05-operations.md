# Operations management

## Transformation and quality

**Slides 3–6:** operations change inputs into outputs that meet customer needs. A process contains smaller processes and activities.

**Campus Tees:** blank shirts, ink and staff time become printed club shirts.

**Slide 6. TQM definition (spelling corrected):**

> An organisation-wide effort to work with quality and continuous improvement (Kaizen)

**Plain English:** everyone helps prevent errors and improve the work. **Kaizen** means ongoing improvement. **BPR** means a major redesign of a business process. **Six Sigma** uses data to reduce defects and variation.

**Campus Tees:** fixing an artwork checklist is a small improvement. Replacing the whole order process is BPR.

**Slide 7.**

| Waste | Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- |
| Overproduction | Print more club designs than customers ordered. |
| Defects | Print the wrong logo. |

| Motion | Staff walk around looking for tools. |

## P-time, D-time and the decoupling point

**Slide 8. Exact definitions:**

> P-Time is the production lead time

> D-Time is the time that the customer is willing to wait

> CODP – Customer Order Decoupling Point, defined as the point where the product is linked to a specific order

**Plain English:** P-time is the work's full lead time. D-time is the buyer's allowed wait. The CODP is where work becomes tied to one real order.

**Campus Tees:** supply takes 5 days, printing 2, delivery 1: P-time is 8 days. A club accepts 3 days. Hold common blanks before the order; print after the club confirms its logo.

## Lean, agile and leagile: Naylor, Naim and Berry

**Assigned paper, p. 108. Exact definitions:**

> Leanness means developing a value stream to eliminate all waste, including time, and to ensure a level schedule.

> Agility means using market knowledge and a virtual corporation to exploit profitable opportunities in a volatile market place.

**Plain English:** lean removes waste and keeps work steady. Agile uses customer information and flexible partners to respond to change. Lean often focuses on low cost; agile often focuses on quick service when demand changes.

**Leagile** combines them. Campus Tees buys common blanks in a steady, low-waste flow. After the CODP, it prints the confirmed design quickly. Both sides need good information and reliable processes.

[diagram:leagile]

**Slide 10. Exact postponement definition:**

> Postponement is a business strategy which maximizes possible benefit and minimizes risk by delaying further investment into a product or service until the last possible moment

**Plain English:** wait for better information before making a product specific. Still meet the promised date.

**From the paper:** keep common parts ready, then finish the required version later. This can reduce wrong finished stock. It needs enough finishing time and capacity.

## Match the method to demand and lead time

**Slide 11:** predictable demand with long lead time suits lean planning. Predictable demand with short lead time suits regular Kanban refills. Unpredictable demand with short lead time suits agile response. Unpredictable demand with long lead time can suit postponement.

## Reduce wasted time and find causes

**Slides 12–16:** a time-based map separates work time from waiting. A cause-and-effect diagram lists possible causes under people, machines, materials and methods. Check the causes with evidence.

**Campus Tees:** earlier artwork approval reduces waiting. A clear size check reduces wrong prints. Less waiting and rework can reduce stock, equipment needs and quality costs. Removing checks without controlling quality can create more defects.
