[answer:Compact answer key]
Needs are felt deprivation; wants are culturally and individually shaped forms; demand adds purchasing power. Industrial marketing serves non-personal purposes and input demand follows downstream demand. STP identifies bases and profiles, evaluates and selects segments, then develops positioning and mixes.

SCM manages upstream/downstream relationships for superior customer value and lower whole-chain cost. Material generally goes downstream and requirements upstream. Integration progresses from baseline to functional, internal and external. The Rs are responsiveness, reliability, resilience and relationships.

Cost advantage concerns relative efficiency; value advantage concerns preferred benefits. Scale concerns output and resource economics; experience concerns cumulative output. Barney assumes heterogeneity and imperfect mobility. VRIN means valuable, rare, imperfectly imitable and non-substitutable. History, ambiguous causation and complex social systems can hinder copying; strategic alternatives can duplicate benefits differently.

ARA means actors/actor bonds, resources/resource ties and activities/activity links. Relationship, small world and wider world are levels. Purchasing issues are scope, supplier-base structure and relationship involvement. Kraljic combines profit impact and supply risk: routine/rationalise, leverage/competition, bottleneck/continuity and strategic/partnership. Its stages are classification, market analysis, positioning and action plans.

MUDA includes overproduction, defects, inventory, motion, overprocessing, transport and waiting. P-time is process lead time; D-time is acceptable customer wait. CODP links the product to a specific order; postponement delays commitment. Leagility combines lean upstream and agile downstream.

EOQ answers quantity, ROP timing and ABC attention by value. ERP integrates, MPS schedules output, MRP plans materials and WMS supports warehouse execution. SKU identifies; VMI assigns vendor replenishment; 3PL executes outsourced logistics and 4PL coordinates the wider network. Distributive divides a pie; integrative can expand it. BATNA is the best fallback action. Phases are preparation, greeting, introduction, argumentation, compromise, closure and realisation.
[/answer]

## Formula sheet with units

| Purpose | Formula | Unit or interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profit margin | Profit / turnover | A proportion or percentage. |
| Asset turnover | Turnover / assets | Times per period. |
| ROI in the DuPont illustration | Margin × asset turnover | Return on the relevant asset base. |
| Contribution per unit | p − v | Currency per unit. |
| Break-even quantity | F / (p − v) | Units, when p > v and the simple assumptions apply. |
| Profit | (p − v)q − F | Currency in the relevant period. |
| Daily demand | Annual demand / working days | Units per working day. |
| Reorder point | dL + SS | Units; match time bases. |
| EOQ | √(2DK/h) | Units per order. |
| Orders per year | D/Q | Orders/year. |
| Average cycle inventory | Q/2 | Units; add constant SS separately if relevant. |
| Annual ordering cost | KD/Q | Currency/year. |
| Annual cycle-stock holding cost | hQ/2 | Currency/year. |
| Total annual EOQ cost | PD + KD/Q + hQ/2 | Currency/year, excluding a separate constant safety-stock term. |
| Annual usage value for ABC | Annual quantity × unit cost | Currency/year. |
| P/D gap | P-time − D-time | A timing gap to bridge if positive. |

D is annual demand, K fixed order cost, h annual holding cost per unit, P unit price and Q order size. F is fixed cost, p selling price, v variable cost per unit and q output. Distinguish uppercase P for purchase price in EOQ notation from P-time in order fulfilment. Symbols are useful only when their meanings and time bases are clear.

## Four final explanations you should be able to give aloud

Explain why a lower purchase price can create a worse supply-chain outcome. Then explain why a common machine does not necessarily create a sustained advantage. Next explain how a supplier relationship can both enable and constrain a company. Finally explain how preparing generic stock can coexist with responding to actual customer orders.

If you can give each explanation with a mechanism and a simple example, you have connected the module's central ideas. Revisit any section where you can recite a term but cannot explain what would actually change in a business.
