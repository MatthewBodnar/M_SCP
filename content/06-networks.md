# Industrial networks

## Buy, make and work closely together

**Slide 3:** ownership and close cooperation are different choices.

| Form | Simple meaning | Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classical market contracting | Buy from an independent firm. | Choose a courier for one delivery. |
| Quasi-vertical integration | Separate firms share some work, costs or risks. | Develop a delivery process with a long-term supplier. |
| Vertical integration | Own the activity. | Run an internal printing and delivery team. |

A firm can work closely with a supplier without owning it.

## IMP interaction model

**Slides 4–5:** repeated exchanges help firms learn about each other and reduce uncertainty.

The model includes **product/service, information, financial and social exchanges**. It also considers the firms and people involved, their relationship's atmosphere and the wider environment.

**Plain English:** what happens depends on the work, the people, their trust and the situation around them.

**Campus Tees:** the wholesaler learns the printer's size codes. Both firms agree on an order deadline. Their routines become linked. This helps delivery, but can make switching suppliers harder.

## ARA: three connected parts

**Slide 6. Keep the model's exact labels.**

| Part | Relationship link | Simple meaning and Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actors | Actor bonds | Who is involved: the printer and wholesaler build trust. |
| Resources | Resource ties | What they use: shirt fabric and print settings are adapted to fit. |
| Activities | Activity links | What they do: dispatch and printing schedules are joined. |

[diagram:ara]

The parts affect each other. Trust can support a fabric trial. The new fabric then changes printing and delivery work.

## Ford and Mouzas: business networking

**Assigned paper, p. 436. Exact definition:**

> Business networking is the conscious problem-driven attempts of one or more business actors to change or develop some aspect(s) of the substance of interaction in relationships in which they and others are involved.

**Plain English:** firms try to change connected business relationships to solve a problem.

**Campus Tees:** late deliveries lead the printer, wholesaler and courier to agree on a new handover time. Networking is more than meeting people.

## Three levels and network pictures

**From Ford and Mouzas.** Do not confuse these levels with ARA's three parts.

| Level | Simple meaning | Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relationship | One direct relationship. | Printer and wholesaler. |
| Small world | Known links around the firm. | The wholesaler's link to a shirt maker. |
| Wider world | More distant actors and changes. | New industry rules that affect several firms. |

[diagram:networklevels]

A **network picture** is how an actor understands the network. It is incomplete. Campus Tees may blame the courier; the courier may see a late warehouse release.

**Main paper point:** one firm cannot fully control the network. Solving a problem may need changes in several relationships. Close links support learning, but can also create dependence.
