# SCM and strategy fundamentals

## Supply chain management and flows

**Slide 2, Christopher (2011). Exact definition:**

> The management of upstream and downstream relationships with suppliers and customers in order to deliver superior customer value at less cost to the supply chain as a whole.

**Plain English:** help suppliers and customers work together. Give customers better value while keeping the whole chain's cost low.

**Campus Tees:** a cheap shirt is a poor choice if late delivery makes the club miss its event.

**Slides 4 and 8:** goods usually move towards the customer. Payments usually move towards suppliers. Orders, forecasts and delivery information move both ways. Returns move goods back.

## Strategy, customer value and the value chain

**Slides 5–7:** ask where the firm is now, where it wants to be, and how it will get there.

Ohmae's three Cs are **customer, company and competitor**. Campus Tees must understand club needs, its own skills and other printers' offers.

Porter's primary activities are **inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service**. Support activities are **infrastructure, human resources, technology and procurement**.

**Plain English:** look at every part of the work. Ask how each part adds value or cost.

## Integration and Christopher's four Rs

**Slide 9:** integration has four stages: separate departments; joined functions; the whole firm working together; the firm working with suppliers and customers.

**Campus Tees:** sales shares confirmed orders with printing. The shirt supplier also sees the delivery plan.

[diagram:integration]

| Four Rs: slides 10–11 | Simple meaning | Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Responsiveness | Respond to a change quickly. | Change the print plan after a club updates its order. |
| Reliability | Do what was promised. | Deliver the correct shirts on Friday. |
| Resilience | Keep working and recover after a problem. | Use a qualified backup supplier. |
| Relationships | Work well with other firms. | Solve delivery problems with the wholesaler. |

**Christopher reading:** customer value and total chain cost belong together. A saving in purchasing can create extra stock, waste or delivery costs elsewhere.

## Barney: resources and sustained advantage

**Reading: Barney (1991), pp. 101–102.** Resources include equipment, people, knowledge and ways of working. **Resource heterogeneity** means firms have different resources. **Imperfect resource mobility** means some resources cannot move easily between firms.

**Exact definition of sustained competitive advantage:**

> A firm is said to have a sustained competitive advantage when it is implementing a value creating strategy not simultaneously being implemented by any current or potential competitors and when these other firms are unable to duplicate the benefits of this strategy.

**Plain English:** the firm creates value in a way rivals do not use. Rivals also cannot copy the benefits.

| VRIN test | Simple question | Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valuable | Does it help the firm meet a need or deal with a threat? | A reliable routine helps meet event deadlines. |
| Rare | Do few rivals have it? | Few printers have the same trusted club network. |
| Imperfectly imitable | Is it hard to copy? | Years of trust and shared experience are hard to buy. |
| Non-substitutable | Is there no easy alternative that gives the same benefit? | Check whether another system can give equal service. |

[diagram:vrin]

Copying may be hard because of **history**, **causal ambiguity** or **social complexity**. These mean a unique past, unclear reasons for success, or complex human relationships. A printer anyone can buy is unlikely to pass all four tests. Sustained does not mean forever.

## Other supply chain terms to know

These terms also appear in the supplied exam papers.

**Bullwhip:** small changes in customer sales cause larger changes in orders upstream. Share real sales data and agree how to order stock.

**SCOR:** a common model for describing, measuring and improving supply chain work. Classic names are Plan, Source, Make, Deliver, Return and Enable. The Digital Standard uses Orchestrate, Plan, Order, Source, Transform, Fulfill and Return. Do not mix the versions.

**Risk:** a supplier failure or wrong demand forecast can stop work. Qualified alternatives, useful buffers and shared plans can reduce the damage. They also cost money.

**Sustainability:** consider the environment, people and long-term business costs across the chain. Campus Tees can reduce unsold prints and work with suppliers to improve working conditions.
