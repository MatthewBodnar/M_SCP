# Purchasing and supply management

## Purchasing and its financial effect

**Slide 3, van Weele (2005:12). Exact definition:**

> The management of the company’s external resources in such a way that the supply of all goods, services, capabilities and knowledge which are necessary for running, maintaining and managing the company’s primary and support activities is secured at the most favourable conditions.

**Plain English:** get the outside goods, services and skills the business needs, on suitable terms. Price is only one part.

**Slide 4: DuPont example.** Sales are 100, purchases 60, other costs 38 and assets 20. Profit is 2. ROI is 2/20 = **10%**.

A 5% saving on purchases reduces them to 57. Profit becomes 5 and ROI becomes **25%**, if everything else stays the same.

**Campus Tees:** cheaper blanks help only if extra defects or late deliveries do not remove the saving.

## Gadde and Håkansson: three strategic choices

**Slide 5 and the assigned paper.**

| Issue | Simple question | Campus Tees example and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Make or buy: scope of supply | Which work should we do ourselves? | Keep key printing skills inside; buy outside transport. Buying can add skills but also dependence. |
| Supplier-base structure | How many suppliers, and how are they linked? | Use a main wholesaler and a qualified backup. More suppliers also need more checking. |
| Nature of relationships | How closely should we work with suppliers? | Share plans for important shirts. Close work takes time and can make switching harder. |

**From the paper:** close relationships can reduce admin and production costs. This is **rationalisation**. They can also support **technical development**, such as testing a better fabric together.

The choices affect each other. Buying more outside work can make supplier skills and close planning more important.

**Single sourcing:** use one supplier for a need. **Multiple sourcing:** use several. **Parallel sourcing:** use different suppliers for different product lines while keeping close relationships within each line.

## Core competence and outsourcing

**Slide 6: core competence** combines skills and resources that help the firm compete. It should open several markets, add customer benefit and be hard to copy.

**Campus Tees:** knowing how to handle custom club orders may matter more than owning a delivery van.

**Slide 7. Exact outsourcing definition:**

> Complete transfer of a business process to an independent external organization

**Plain English:** another firm takes over a business process.

**Slides 8–12:** outsourcing can lower costs, provide skills and free time for core work. Hidden costs include quality problems, supplier management, internal coordination and setting up the new arrangement.

## Kraljic's purchasing matrix

**Slides 13–14 and Kraljic (1983).** The axes are **profit impact** and **supply risk**.

| Category | Impact / risk | Main action | Campus Tees example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Routine / non-critical | Low / low | Simplify buying. | Standard office paper. |
| Leverage | High / low | Compare qualified offers and use buying volume. | Common blanks bought in large amounts. |
| Bottleneck | Low / high | Protect supply; consider a suitable buffer. | A low-cost spare from one supplier. |
| Strategic | High / high | Plan closely and manage a long-term relationship. | A special fabric central to the main product. |
